# The Center of CX. Brand Guide 1.0

The standard for how The Center of CX looks, reads, moves and speaks. Adopted 26 September 2026 by TB after five design rounds
(archived on the design canvas). This file governs; the canvas boards illustrate it. Where they differ, this file wins until it is
revised under section 17.

---

## Manifesto

Customer experience technology is bought with other people's money and lived with by other people's teams. Too much of what
buyers read is written to sell. We exist so the person making the decision can see what is true before anyone sends a proposal.

1. **Diagnose before you buy.** The first answer is about the buyer's own operation. The market comes second.
2. **Show the working.** Every figure says where it came from. Every formula is published. Every assumption is labelled and can
   be replaced with the reader's own.
3. **Say how sure we are.** Every result carries its grade, names what holds it down, and says what would raise it. When a number
   cannot be trusted, we show no number.
4. **Buy nothing is an answer.** Every route ends with an honest exit, printed in the same type as every other outcome.
5. **Independence is the product.** Vendors are listed, never ranked for sale, never scored for pay. Market news never changes research.
6. **The industry speaks here.** Practitioners publish and are credited. Their perspective is labelled as theirs, with any tie disclosed.
7. **Simple enough for the floor, serious enough for the boardroom.** One question per screen. Plain words first. Depth one tap away.
8. **Made by people who know the work.** Nothing here should read as a template or as generated filler. If it does, it is not finished.

## 3. Principles and voice

Five rules for every screen:

1. **One question per screen.** Doors on the homepage, one input group at a time in tools, the answer appears as you type.
2. **Nothing starts empty.** Every field starts filled and says where its value came from. Press nothing, still get an answer.
3. **One obvious next step.** One primary button per screen. The exit to buy nothing always in view.
4. **Plain words first.** No acronym on first sight. Labels are questions. Buttons say what happens.
5. **Depth one tap away.** Methods, sources and grades are there for the sceptic and out of the way for the newcomer.

Voice: the voice of the industry, edited by an independent analyst. Outcome first, short sentences, plain words.

- No em dash or en dash, anywhere. No "X, not Y" cadence in public copy.
- Retired words: most conservative, only independent, survives the CFO, industry-leading, best-in-class, world-class, seamless,
  cutting-edge, game-changing, revolutionary, state-of-the-art, unparalleled, unmatched.
- Every displayed number is one of four claim classes: historical fact, assumption, conditional forecast, measured outcome.
  "Under these assumptions this models $X" is the form. "This saves $X" is forbidden.
- Cut words by half on every draft. Example: "Enter your fully loaded average hourly wage including benefits for front-line
  agents" becomes "What do you pay an agent an hour?"

## 4. The mark

Three arcs converge on an X: everything meets at the center. The gap on the right is the way in. Weight and opacity step down
outward so the eye travels to the decision.

- Construction: arcs of radius 30, 44 and 58 on a 120 unit box, opening 120 degrees to the right; weights 5, 3.2 and 2 (display)
  at 100, 70 and 45 percent; the X in #00AAFF, arms 26, weight 5.5.
- Lockups: mist arcs with #00AAFF X on navy or ink; navy arcs with #0072BB X on mist or paper. Wordmark in IBM Plex Sans SemiBold.
- App icon on #13284A with a 26 radius. Below 24 pixels, one heavy arc and the X.
- Clear space equals the inner arc radius on every side.
- Never close the rings, rotate the mark, recolour the everyday mark, replace the X, or set it on a bright field.

## 5. Special editions

The everyday mark never changes. On set dates the arcs carry colours a community or occasion already owns, then return to mist.

- Current editions: Pillar edition (magenta, amber and teal arcs, #00AAFF X) for anniversaries, launch weeks and events;
  CX Day and Customer Service Week in October; Pride in June; Earth Day on 22 April.
- Calendar to confirm each year: Lunar New Year, Black History Month (US), Women's History Month, Earth Day, AANHPI Heritage Month
  (US), Pride Month and Juneteenth, Disability Pride Month, Hispanic Heritage Month (US, 15 Sep to 15 Oct), CX Day and Customer
  Service Week, Veterans Day, International Day of Persons with Disabilities, our anniversary, a pillar launch.
- Drawing a new edition: use colours the community recognises and ask someone from it before publishing; three arcs and the X
  only, no symbols, mascots or slogans; check every arc is visible on navy and say so if a colour is lightened; set start and end
  dates, a changelog entry and one line on why we mark it.
- Where: header, favicon, social avatars, newsletter masthead, share cards, event slides. Never on a result, report, method page
  or vendor page.

## 7. The evidence mark and the readout

One family of arcs, two instruments that never look alike.

- **Evidence mark: how sure.** Always three arcs with fixed colours: Evidence #6CC8FF, Realization #0088DD, Completeness #EAF0F7.
  Colour names the question, never the answer. A not applicable axis draws as a dotted ring. A void result shows no mark and no
  figure. Small, labelled "How sure", on every figure-producing tool and report. The headline grade is the weakest axis, and the
  line beneath names it. On paper the arcs print Evidence #2F8FD0, Realization #0072BB, Completeness #0B1D3A on a #E4E9EF
  track, each at 3:1 or better against white; a not applicable axis is a dotted #5B6B80 ring.
- **Grades** (Directional, Planning-grade, Finance-grade) carry weight and fill, never colour. A grade describes evidence and
  never grades the reader.
- **Readout: your result.** Assessments only. One thin arc per part of the assessment, the score where the X would be, labelled
  "Your result", placed large at the top. Arc colours follow the stack layer each part belongs to. It moves as the reader answers
  and shows no score until every part is answered.

tables. Plex Mono for formulas. Tabular numerals everywhere.

| Style | Size and weight |
|---|---|
| Figure | 96 Bold, tracking minus 3 percent |
| Display | 72 Bold |
| Heading 1 | 44 Bold |
| Heading 2 | 28 SemiBold |

## 10. Icons

Drawn like the mark: 24 pixel grid, 2 pixel stroke, round caps and joins, no fills, one colour that follows the text, shown at 20
or 24. Inline SVG built in-house: no icon font, no outside host, no emoji. Every icon sits beside a word except close, menu and
search. Pillar icons take pillar colour only in the nav and doors. The set of 32: diagnostics, vendors, industries, research,
market watch, evidence, layers, method, search, next, download, print, link, share, external, pulled, info, unknown, critical,
clear, exit, contributor, person, lock, calendar, time, filter, add, less, close, menu, check.

## 11. Space, radius, depth, motion

- Space on a base of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 72. Components 8 to 24, sections 48 to 88, phone gutter 16.
- Radius: 6 chips, 12 fields and buttons, 18 cards, pills for toggles.
- Depth is earned: flat by default; only the result and the route card float.
- Texture: contour lines and a faint grid in CSS, 4 to 7 percent. No photos, no video backgrounds, no gradient washes.
- Motion: 180 ms press and select; 450 ms figures counting and layers parting; 1,600 ms arcs drawing once. One curve,
  cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1). Nothing loops for attention. Reduced motion shows the end state.
- Touch targets 44 pixels or larger. No sideways page scroll at 390 pixels.

## 13. Page patterns

- **Homepage.** "Diagnose before you buy." Step 1: where would you like to begin (five doors). Step 2: the question that door
  needs, then a route with a possible ending. The stack lifts the layer the route touches.
- **Tool.** Route sidebar (layer, steps done, you are here, next); inputs prefilled with sources; live result with the evidence
  mark; analyst read; one next test; the exit. On a phone the result and grade are pinned at the top and the next step at the bottom.
- **Report.** Light paper, two pages for a calculator. Page 1: the answer, how sure, what it means for the reader, what would
  change it, how to raise the grade, next test. Page 2: every input and its source, the working in Plex Mono, each axis, limits,
  scenario and method links. The reader chooses who it is written for before download.
- **Vendor profile.** Name, category, research status and validation date; the corpus one line; competitive class; best when,
  caution when, eliminate when; findings by research domain with capability and evidence state; where it breaks; what it takes to
  run; what to prove and what to put in the contract; products and control planes; sources with dates. Test it yourself, same class
  A to Z, Market Watch (separate), contributor perspectives.
- **Industry page.** Sourced figures with claim markers, regulation, failure modes, the seven layer capability map, sources list.
- **Method page.** Formulas in words and Plex Mono, constants with kind and source, worked example, checked against, changelog.
- **States.** Loading keeps the final shape; void explains which input made a number impossible; failures offer one way on; empty
  search suggests categories; half-finished assessments show no score; shared scenarios say whose numbers they are.

## 14. Research surfaces

- Vendor Intelligence shows research truth only: current, validated, publishable claims from the corpus. Phase 1 scores, tiers,
  score deltas and Market Position values never appear on a vendor page.
- Numeric Phase 2 ratings are locked; no page shows a score, rank, tier or composite for any vendor.
- Only evidence marked publishable renders. Anything marked internal renders nothing, not even its existence.
- Unknown is not weak: an unverified area says so and becomes a question to prove.
- Maturity states are shown as they are: generally available, preview, early access, roadmap. Roadmap never earns credit.
- Comparison happens inside a competitive class only. Lists are alphabetical.
- Vendor logos are not shown. Names only.

## 15. Performance and accessibility

- Core Web Vitals at "good" on a mid-range phone over 4G: largest paint under 2.5 s, layout shift under 0.1, input response
  under 200 ms.
- Stack and arcs are CSS and SVG. On phones the stack is still until tapped; glows drop below 600 pixels wide.
- Every chart has a text equivalent, every control a name, focus always visible, contrast AA or better.
- Full-page prerender stays; tools render their default answer on the server so a shared link shows a real number first.
- Reports switch to the paper theme on print with designed page breaks. Emails use system fonts and one column.

## 16. What this guide does not change

Doctrine, the claim classes, the confidence taxonomy, the research operating law (CLAUDE.md section 13) and the security policy
govern content and behaviour. This guide governs presentation. Where a design choice would change what a number means, the
doctrine wins and the design changes.
