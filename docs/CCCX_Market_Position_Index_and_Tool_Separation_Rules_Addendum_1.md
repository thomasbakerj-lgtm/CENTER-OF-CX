# **1. Why This Addendum Exists**

V3 removed the public universal vendor leaderboard. That decision was correct and stands. It created one unresolved problem: it also removed the single largest organic acquisition surface in this market. Buyers search for leaders, top platforms, rankings and comparisons in volume, every day, and will continue to. Abandoning that demand does not make the demand more sophisticated. It hands it to review aggregators, analyst firms and vendor marketing.

The resolution is a second public surface with a different name, a different definition, a different input set and a hard architectural wall between it and Vendor Match.

The Market Position Index answers the question the market already asks. Vendor Match answers the question that actually decides the purchase. The distance between those two answers is the product thesis, made visible on the page where the traffic lands.

## **1.1 What this addendum changes**

- It reinstates a public ranked market view, renamed and redefined as the Market Position Index.

- It defines exactly what the index measures, what it must never measure, and how it must be displayed.

- It establishes the separation law: the index never feeds Vendor Match, in any direction, through any field.

- It requires the index to deliver intelligence rather than a score table. A ranked row without a market read is not a deliverable.

- It defines the ingestion rules for the future corpus: white papers, product documentation, demo recordings, call transcripts and field evidence.

- It generalizes the separation law into a rule set that governs every tool built on the platform from this point forward.

## **1.2 What this addendum does not change**

- Vendor Match remains the only place where a buyer-specific ranking is produced.

- No universal purchase recommendation is published anywhere on the platform.

- Capability, Evidence Confidence, Buyer Fit, Production and Operating Risk, and Implementation and Change remain separate layers.

- Unknown does not equal weak. Known does not equal strong.

- Competitive classes remain mandatory. Unlike vendors are not forced into one peer set.

- Independence is absolute. No sponsorship, commercial relationship or advisory engagement influences index position, match order, evidence state or research language.

# **2. Three Public Surfaces, Three Different Truths**

The platform now presents three public surfaces powered by one research corpus. Each answers a different question and produces a different kind of truth. Confusing them is the primary failure mode this document exists to prevent.

| **Surface** | **Question it answers** | **Truth produced** | **Primary job** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Market Position Index | Who is established in this market, what do they sell, and how did they get here? | Market truth | Acquisition, orientation, education, routing into vendor research and Vendor Match. |
| Vendor Intelligence | What is this platform, what can it do, where does it break, what does it take to implement and own? | Research truth | Authoritative vendor-by-vendor decision intelligence. System of record for Vendor Match. |
| Vendor Match | Which platforms are rational for this buyer, in what order, and what is this buyer signing up for? | Buyer truth | Contextual ranking, consequence explanation, proof burden, advisory conversion. |

| **SEPARATION LAW** Market truth describes the vendor. Buyer truth describes the relationship between the vendor and one specific environment. Market truth may be displayed alongside buyer truth. Market truth may never be an input to buyer truth. |
| --- |

# **3. The Market Position Index: Definition**

The Market Position Index is an ordered public view of how vendors currently position within a defined category and competitive class. It measures presence, breadth, adoption evidence, ecosystem and operating maturity. It is a measure of how established a vendor is. It carries no assertion about whether the vendor is the right choice for any individual buyer.

## **3.1 Naming rules**

The construct name carries the meaning. Naming discipline is not cosmetic here. The moment the page reads as a buying recommendation, V3 collapses and the platform becomes another listicle.

| **Permitted** | **Forbidden** |
| --- | --- |
| 2026 CCaaS Market Position Index | Best CCaaS Platforms |
| CCaaS Market Presence and Product Breadth Ranking | Top CCaaS Vendors Ranked |
| Market Position: 2nd | Overall Score: 89 |
| Leading market presence in this class | Our number two pick |
| Best known for | Best for you |
| Established Presence | Recommended |
| This vendor ranks second on market presence in this class | This vendor is the second best option |

*Page titles, meta descriptions and H1 tags may legitimately target the language buyers search. The construct name and the on-page assertions may not. A page may be titled to answer the query **"**top CCaaS vendors**"** and then immediately explain that market position is not buyer fit. That is honest and it is the whole point of the page.*

## **3.2 Scored dimensions**

Five dimensions are scored. Each is rated on the Presence Scale, a five-point scale that mirrors the V3 Capability State structure so researchers work in one mental model.

| **Presence Scale** | **Meaning** |
| --- | --- |
| 0 Minimal | Little or no evidence of presence on this dimension within the category. |
| 1 Limited | Present but narrow, early, regional or single-segment. |
| 2 Established | Credible and repeatable presence at normal category scale. |
| 3 Broad | Substantial presence across segments, geographies or product scope. |
| 4 Extensive | Among the largest or broadest in the category on this dimension. |

| **Dimension** | **What it measures** | **Permitted evidence** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market Footprint | Customer scale, deployment count, seats or users where validated, category-attributable revenue where disclosed, geographic reach. | Company disclosures, filings, press releases, earnings materials, verified customer counts, official regional availability documentation. |
| Customer Evidence | Volume, recency and breadth of public customer proof. Named references, case studies, review counts and review recency across platforms. | Published case studies, named logos with dated sources, review platform counts and posting recency. Counts only, never review content. |
| Product and Solution Breadth | Breadth of generally available functionality relevant to the category, and the adjacent modules the vendor can sell into the same account. | Official product documentation, pricing and packaging pages, release notes, feature availability matrices published by the vendor. |
| Ecosystem and Interoperability | Integrations, certified partners, marketplace presence, API surface, developer resources, implementation partner network. | Marketplace listings, partner directories, published API documentation, certification program pages. |
| Commercial and Operating Maturity | Enterprise availability, support footprint, geographic and language capability, security and compliance breadth, demonstrated operating longevity, ownership stability. | Trust and compliance centers, certification registries, support documentation, corporate history, ownership and funding records. |

## **3.3 Displayed but never scored**

Independent Coverage is shown on the vendor row as dated fact. It is not scored and it does not affect order.

| **Element** | **Treatment** | **Reason** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Analyst inclusion or recognition | Cite the fact of inclusion with the firm name, report name and date. Never reproduce rankings, placements, figures, quadrant positions or text. | Scoring coverage borrows another firm authority and builds an incumbency loop. Coverage tracks company size and briefing program investment, not product merit. |
| Press and industry coverage | Cite notable dated coverage where it establishes a market fact. | Useful orientation. Not a measure of product or fit. |
| Awards and certifications | List with issuing body and date. | Frequently pay-to-enter. Informational only. |

## **3.4 Excluded inputs**

These are excluded by rule. A researcher who cannot complete a dimension without one of these leaves the dimension unrated and records why.

| **Excluded input** | **Why** |
| --- | --- |
| Estimated or derived market share | True apples-to-apples share is unavailable across private vendors with different product definitions and reporting periods. Estimating it introduces exactly the weak evidence the Research Operating Standard exists to eliminate. |
| Reproduced analyst rankings or placements | Borrowed authority, likely intellectual property exposure, and it makes our index a derivative of someone else work. |
| Star ratings and average review scores | Review populations and incentives differ by platform and by vendor program. Averages are not comparable across vendors. |
| Review content, quotes or excerpts | Third-party content. Counts and recency only. |
| Sponsorship, partnership, advisory relationship or commercial interest | Absolute prohibition. Independence is the product. |
| Web traffic, social following, conference presence, SEO visibility | Measures marketing spend, not market position. |
| Any Vendor Match output or buyer session data | Directional contamination. See Section 5. |
| Any Phase 2 atomic capability finding | Capability belongs to research truth and buyer truth. It does not belong to market truth. See Section 5. |

# **4. Display Rules: Rank Without False Precision**

The index is ordered. It does not publish a composite decimal score. A composite implies the evidence separates vendors at a precision the inputs cannot support, which is the same false-confidence failure V3 prohibits on Vendor Match, relocated to a new page.

| **WORKING FORMULA. PUBLISHED, NOT HIDDEN.** Index Position Value = sum of the five Presence Scale ratings, equally weighted, out of a maximum of 20. Order descending. Where two vendors are within one point, mark them as a near-tie and present them as a tied position. The formula, the weights and the rating definitions are published on the methodology page. |
| --- |

| **Rule** | **Implementation** |
| --- | --- |
| Show components, not just order | Every row exposes the five dimension ratings. The buyer can see what created the position and can disagree with it. |
| Use position bands | Group into Leading Presence, Established Presence, Focused Presence and Emerging Presence. Bands communicate more honestly than ordinals and survive minor input changes without churn. |
| Expose near-ties | Where the component sum differs by one point or less, display a shared position. Do not manufacture separation. |
| Date every input | Each dimension rating carries a last-validated date. Stale ratings are flagged, never silently presented as current. |
| Refresh quarterly | Category index refresh on a quarterly cycle, with event-driven updates on acquisition, product retirement, major release or ownership change. |
| Scope to competitive class | Publish index positions within a competitive class. A specialist and a full suite are not ranked against each other on breadth as though they were attempting the same job. |
| Publish the methodology | One methodology page per category index, linked from every row. Formula, definitions, sources, exclusions, refresh cadence and known limitations. |
| Disclose the size correlation | State plainly on the methodology page that four of the five scored dimensions correlate with company scale, and that the index will therefore closely resemble a size ranking. Claiming five-dimension nuance when scale drives the order is the same false-confidence failure in a new location. |

# **5. The Separation Law**

This section is a hard constraint on the build. It is testable and it should be enforced in code and in the test suite, not by convention.

| **Rule** | **Statement** | **Test** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1. One-way wall | No Market Position Index value, component rating, band or ordinal may be read by the Vendor Match engine for any purpose: scoring, weighting, ordering, tie-breaking, default sorting, candidate pool construction or result presentation order. | Static check. The Vendor Match module must not import, reference or receive any index field. Rail report must show zero index keys pulled by the match engine. |
| R2. No reverse contamination | No Vendor Match session output, aggregate match statistic, elimination rate or buyer input may adjust an index position. | Index computation takes only the five dated dimension ratings. No session data in the input set. |
| R3. Shared corpus, separate capture | Both surfaces draw from one research corpus. They draw different fields. Index fields are public, cheap and market-level. Match fields are atomic, evidence-stated and buyer-relevant. | Every corpus field is tagged with its permitted consumers. A field tagged index-only cannot be read by the match engine and the reverse holds. |
| R4. Facts move, verdicts do not | A market fact that creates a genuine buyer consequence may be researched independently as a Phase 2 claim and evaluated on its own evidence. The index rating that summarized it never transfers. | Example: global support footprint may matter to a buyer with 24-hour multi-region operations. The researcher evaluates support coverage as an atomic claim with its own evidence state. The vendor does not receive match credit because it scored 4 on Commercial and Operating Maturity. |
| R5. No self-credentialing | No tool may credential itself or another tool from its own published output. Existing data-rail doctrine applies without exception to the index. | getExternalPrimitive enforcement. railReport orphanPulls empty before any surface locks. |
| R6. Disclosure on both surfaces | Each surface states what it measures, what it does not measure, and where the other answer lives. | Index row links to Vendor Match. Match result links to vendor research. Neither claims the other authority. |

| **WHY R1 IS ABSOLUTE** Scale is the single most seductive corruption path into a fit engine. Every commercial pressure, every intuition and every convenience argument points toward letting the big vendor place higher in the match. One weighting decision converts the decision engine into a size ranking with extra steps, and nobody outside the build would be able to detect it. |
| --- |

# **6. The Index Must Deliver Intelligence**

A ranked table of scores is a commodity. It will not earn links, it will not differentiate in generative search, and it will not convert. The index is an intelligence surface that happens to be ordered. Every row carries a market read that no aggregator publishes.

This is also the cheapest intelligence in the program. It comes from public sources, it does not require Phase 2 depth, and it can be produced at volume while the deep vendor research proceeds category by category.

| **Row element** | **Required content** |
| --- | --- |
| Market position and band | Ordinal position within competitive class, band label, and the five component ratings. |
| Competitive class | What kind of platform this is and which vendors are rational peers. |
| What they sell | Product and solution set. Core platform plus adjacent modules the vendor can sell into the same account. This section expands as the corpus grows. |
| Best known for | The one or two things the market genuinely associates with this vendor. |
| Who they serve | Typical segment, vertical concentration, geographic strength, operating model fit patterns observable from public evidence. |
| Lineage and ownership | Corporate owner, ownership status, acquisition history, product lineage, surviving architecture, migration state, renamed or retired products. |
| Recent market moves | Dated acquisitions, launches, retirements, leadership changes, funding events, strategic shifts. |
| Market caveat | The honest limitation of the market view for this vendor. Example: broad suite breadth is concentrated in one region, or footprint is inherited from an acquisition still mid-migration. |
| Buyer fit statement | Fixed language. Buyer fit depends on your requirements. Position does not determine fit. |
| Routing | Link to the vendor research page and a direct call to action into Vendor Match. |

## **6.1 Category-level intelligence on every index page**

The index page is also the category orientation page. Above or beside the ranked table it carries the market read that makes the page non-commodity.

- Competitive class definitions and why the classes exist in this category.

- Consolidation and ownership map. Who bought whom, what survived, what was retired.

- Architecture shifts underway in the category and what they change for buyers.

- What the market ranking cannot tell you. The category-specific factors that actually decide these purchases.

- Routing into the diagnostic tools and Vendor Match.

# **7. Corpus Expansion: White Papers, Documentation, Demos and Transcripts**

The corpus will expand beyond public web research into vendor white papers, product documentation, recorded demos, briefing sessions, RFP responses and field conversations. This materially raises research quality. It also introduces confidentiality, consent and evidence-integrity exposure that must be governed before the first document is loaded.

| **Source type** | **Permitted use** | **Publication rule** | **Maximum Evidence State** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public product documentation | Full research use. Primary source for capability and breadth. | Publishable with citation and retrieval date. | Strongly Supported. Verified where the documentation is definitive and current. |
| Vendor white paper or marketing collateral | Generates claims and research questions. | Publishable as a vendor claim, visibly labelled as vendor-stated. | Claimed. Rises only on independent corroboration. |
| Recorded demo or briefing, vendor supplied | Internal research, claim generation, proof question design. | Not publishable without written vendor permission for the specific claim. Never publish the recording. | Inferred to Strongly Supported internally. Publishable only as a verified claim once permitted or independently corroborated. |
| RFP or RFI response | Internal research and pattern detection. | Never published. Never attributed. Frequently confidential by contract. | Internal only. Generates proof questions. |
| Buyer or customer call transcript | Requirement patterns, break hypotheses, Day-2 ownership reality, implementation friction, question design. | Never published. Never attributed. No vendor claim is published from a private conversation without explicit consent. | Internal only. May generate a published pattern once corroborated by three independent sources and stated as a pattern, not as an attributed fact. |
| Analyst report | Fact of inclusion only. | Cite firm, report and date. Never reproduce rankings, text, figures or placements. | Informational. Never scored. |
| Field or channel intelligence | Hypothesis generation and proof question design. | Never published as evidence. Never attributed to a named individual or employer. | Inferred. Must be independently validated before it influences any published finding. |

| **INGESTION RULE** Every ingested artifact enters the corpus with a source tier, a retrieval or session date, a confidentiality flag and a publication permission state. An artifact with no confidentiality determination is treated as confidential until determined. Nothing ingested changes an existing rating until it passes through the Claim Migration Ledger and the Evidence Ledger. |
| --- |

## **7.1 Where the expanded corpus lands**

- Product and solution set detail on the index row. Breadth becomes documented rather than asserted.

- Atomic capability claims in Vendor Intelligence, each with its own evidence state.

- Break records and consequence records, which are the highest-value and hardest-to-source elements of the program.

- Proof questions, which are directly generated from unresolved or vendor-stated claims.

- Nothing lands in the Market Position Index scoring set. Depth of capability is research truth. The index measures presence.

# **8. Universal Tool Rules**

These rules govern the index, Vendor Match, every diagnostic and calculator currently live, and every tool built from this point forward. They exist because the platform value is a chain of independent conclusions drawn from a shared factual baseline. A single undeclared dependency breaks the chain and the failure is silent.

- Declare the truth type. Every tool declares in its specification whether it produces market truth, research truth, buyer truth or operational truth. A tool that produces two truth types is two tools.

- Pass facts, never verdicts. Tools share a factual baseline over the data rail. No tool receives another tool conclusion, grade, rank or recommendation as an input.

- One-way rails, declared. Every cross-tool dependency is explicit, directional and testable. Undeclared reads fail the rail report.

- No self-credentialing. A tool may not read its own published output to credential itself.

- Confidence travels separately. Evidence confidence is never blended into a capability, fit or position value. Uncertainty raises proof burden. It does not become a hidden penalty.

- Publish the methodology. Every scored output has a public methodology page stating the formula, the inputs, the exclusions, the refresh cadence and the known limitations.

- Date everything. Every rating and material claim carries a last-validated date and a refresh trigger. Stale is flagged, never silently presented as current.

- No precision beyond the evidence. Expose ties. Prefer bands and ordering over decimals. Ceiling saturation, where multiple subjects reach the maximum, is a signal of model failure and triggers a methodology review rather than a display fix.

- Each tool serves its own goal. No generic shared ranges or one-size logic imposed across tools.

- Independence is non-negotiable. No commercial relationship influences any output, ordering, evidence state or language, on any surface, ever.

# **9. Instructions for the Research Collaborator**

There are now two capture sets. They are different in depth, cost and purpose. Do not blend them and do not let index work slow down Phase 2 work.

|  | **Market Position capture** | **Phase 2 vendor capture** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Public market intelligence and index position. | Buyer decision intelligence and Vendor Match. |
| Depth | Shallow and broad. Every qualified vendor in the category. | Deep and atomic. Calibration vendors first, then normalization gates roughly every five vendors. |
| Sources | Public only. Documentation, disclosures, filings, marketplaces, registries, case studies, review counts. | Full corpus. Public sources plus documentation, demos, briefings and validated field evidence under Section 7 rules. |
| Output | Five dimension ratings with dates, plus the market read fields in Section 6. | Atomic claims with Applicability, Capability, Evidence, Proof and Decision Effect. Break records, consequence records, implementation gravity, Day-2 ownership, change economics, proof requirements. |
| Time per vendor | Target one to two hours. | Target the full Phase 2 record. Substantially higher. |
| Sequencing | Can run ahead of and in parallel with deep research. Builds the public surface while depth accumulates. | Follows the locked category execution order. CCaaS first. |
| Hard rule | Never rate a dimension on inference. Unrated with a stated reason is correct and acceptable. | Never let a missing field become a negative finding. Unknown does not equal weak. |

## **9.1 Non-negotiables for both capture sets**

- Every rating and claim carries a source, a retrieval date and a last-validated date.

- Unverifiable means unrated with a stated reason. It never means zero and it never means weak.

- Never reproduce analyst text, review content or third-party ranking output.

- Confidential material is flagged at ingestion and never published.

- Phase 1 workbooks and scores remain historical baselines and hypothesis sources. They are not current evidence unless revalidated.

- Framework changes happen at calibration gates and are versioned. They are not introduced silently, vendor by vendor.

# **10. Search Architecture and Honest Expectations**

The index is built for acquisition and internal linking. It is not expected to win the most competitive head terms and the plan does not depend on it doing so. Review aggregators, analyst firms and vendor pages hold those queries with a decade of domain authority. Build the index because the page is legitimately useful, because it completes the category surface, and because it routes qualified traffic into Vendor Match. Treat any head-term ranking as upside.

The winnable surface is the one nobody else can write, and it is a direct byproduct of the Phase 2 research already underway.

| **Query class** | **Example** | **Competitive reality** | **Our surface** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Head ranking terms | Best CCaaS platforms 2026. Top CCaaS vendors. | Held by aggregators and analysts. Hard to win, high volume, low intent. | Market Position Index. Build it, expect slow gains, use it for routing. |
| Head-to-head comparison | Vendor A vs Vendor B. | Contested but winnable with genuinely better content. | Compare surface built on the same research corpus. |
| Vendor-specific intent | Vendor A weaknesses. Where Vendor B breaks. Vendor C implementation cost. | Weakly served. Mostly vendor-controlled or thin. | Vendor Intelligence and Where They Break. High priority. |
| Constraint and requirement | CCaaS with EU data residency. Best platform for a Salesforce architecture. Which platform is easiest to administer. | Poorly served. High intent. Low competition. | Requirement-scoped pages routing directly into Vendor Match. Highest priority. |
| Consequence and operations | CCaaS implementation complexity. Who owns routing changes. Day-2 administration burden. | Almost entirely unserved. | Consequence research. The strongest differentiation the corpus produces. |
| Generative search | Conversational and multi-part buyer questions. | Rewards original, structured, dated, source-backed expert content. Penalizes commodity aggregation. | The structured research corpus is the asset. No other site in this market has one. |

# **11. Acceptance Criteria**

These are build tests, not aspirations. A build that fails any of these has not implemented this addendum.

- The Vendor Match engine contains no reference to any Market Position Index field. Verified by static check and by rail report.

- No public surface displays a composite index score as a decimal or as a single overall number presented as a purchase verdict.

- Every index row displays all five component ratings, each with a last-validated date.

- Near-ties are displayed as tied positions rather than separated by ordinal.

- Every index row carries the market read fields and a direct call to action into Vendor Match.

- Every index page links to a methodology page stating the formula, inputs, exclusions, refresh cadence and the size-correlation disclosure.

- No analyst ranking, review content or third-party ranking text is reproduced anywhere on the platform.

- Index positions are scoped to competitive class, never across classes.

- A vendor holding the top index position can and does appear as eliminated or low-ranked in a Vendor Match session where the buyer requirements justify it, with the reason shown.

- Every ingested corpus artifact carries source tier, date, confidentiality flag and publication permission state.

- No published output uses inferred evidence as if it were verified, on either surface.
